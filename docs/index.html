What the picture shows, for people and for AI. Every count matches the repo.

An open box labelled claude-setup-guide: a Claude Code setup, mistakes included, the part I'd hand to a friend. In the picture, round characters are agents (models) and square robots are hooks (code). The letter on a hat is the model: O Opus, S Sonnet, H Haiku.

  1. CLAUDE.md loads in every session. Rules live there, facts in knowledge/, procedures in skills/, corrections in memory/.
  2. 5 agents, one craft each: builder, breaker, organizer, navigator and skimmer.
  3. 3 guard hooks, because prose explains and a hook enforces: gh-guard.sh, pkill-guard.py, workflow-guard.py.
  4. 35 habits in memory/, one per file; 4 notes in knowledge/, with INDEX.md read first; 2 skills: pr-review-loop and see-the-data.
  5. Check 1, no length cap on a review: the breaker reports every finding, ordered by damage. Brevity is for the verdict line only.
  6. Check 2, the memory index loads whole: MEMORY.md is 55 lines, about 5 KB, well under the budget of 200 lines and ~20 KB. A new line pays with an old one.
  7. Check 3, a reviewer that didn't write the code: a bot reviewer and the breaker loop until both are green, then a cold review in Opus runs the tests once and, on a risky change, plants 10–20 small bugs. Then a person reads it and merges.
  8. Check 4, a guard on the way to main: gh-guard.sh blocks a merge without a human approval, merging through the API, and a push straight to main in your org's repos. Its 25 tests pass.
