**When more than two options compete for the same decision, they go in a table, and every option is labeled the same way everywhere it appears.** Rows are options, columns are the axes the decider named, and the recommended row is marked.

**The pattern:** someone choosing among many options on several criteria at once gets lost in prose and decides quickly once the options are in a grid. Mockups without titles on the image, listed in a different order in the message, make the reader guess which one is A.

**Why:** people compare in grids, not paragraphs. An unlabeled option costs the decider a guess on every look, which is exactly the load a mockup is supposed to remove.

**How to apply:**
- The axes are the decider's, not yours. If they said "cost", "risk" and "time", those are the columns.
- A rejected option keeps its row, with the reason in a cell. What fell out and why is information too.
- Numbers aligned, no decoration.
- On a visual: a big title per option ("A · three lines", "B · one line", "Today") and a plain-words label per row or case. Only show cases where the options differ; drop or caption rows that look identical.
- In the message: same order and same names as the image, one line each (what it is, what it costs, which one is recommended).
- Expect mixes ("B, but with A's link") and offer "mix" as a valid answer instead of forcing one letter.
