**A fact that passes from one session to another, or to a person, carries where it came from and when, or is marked as second-hand.** If whoever relays it did not open the source, they say so.

**The pattern:** facts passed between sessions as prose get corrected later, after some have already reached a person: a date that was stale, a page said not to exist that did, the wrong owner named for a request. The one check that works is verifying a relayed claim against its source before passing it on.

**Why:** prose loses its provenance at every hop, and a confident sentence reads the same whether it came from a query or a guess.

**How to apply:**
- Each fact carries its source on the same line: a link, a `file:line`, a query, or "who said it, where, when". Example: `the shift change already started (team lead, #general, yesterday)`.
- Did not open it yourself? Mark it: `(second-hand: <who told you>)`.
- Before a second-hand fact goes to a person, or to two or more sessions, open the source once. If you can't, it goes marked.
- A correction goes to the same recipients as the original, with its source.
- Not needed for your own reasoning, a plan, or a number already in a committed file; cite the file.
- Check owners against the system's owner field, not memory.
